# Sources & Grounding — Project 01

**The dataset in this folder is simulated.** No real plant data is used. The *structure, behaviour, and problem framing* are grounded in the published turnaround-management literature below. Cite these when you defend the project — they show the problem is real and well-studied.

## Why this problem is real (benchmarking evidence)
- **More than two-thirds of turnarounds** exceed planned cost or schedule by 10%+, or suffer a trip shortly after start-up.
- **~40% of turnarounds** experience a cost overrun or schedule delay of **more than 30%**.
- Typical cost overruns average **15–20%**, and schedule delays affect **nearly half** of all turnarounds.
- **Work scope (and scope growth during execution) is the single biggest driver** of turnaround cost, schedule, and reliability outcomes.
- Asset Performance Networks (**AP-Networks**) maintains a benchmarking database of **1,350+ turnaround observations**.
- **Independent Project Analysis (IPA)** notes over half of sites fail to integrate turnarounds with capital projects run in the same window — a known overrun mechanism.

## Real-world context for your narrative
Refinery **turnarounds (TARs / shutdowns)** are planned, periodic shutdowns of a process unit — every ~4–6 years per unit, lasting 3–6 weeks — to inspect, repair, replace and clean equipment that cannot be serviced while running. They are typically the **single largest maintenance cost event** a refinery manages, and among the highest-risk for both budget and safety. This makes overrun analysis a genuinely high-value analyst problem. Sarnia's "Chemical Valley" (Imperial Oil, Shell, Suncor, NOVA Chemicals) runs exactly these events — a credible local anchor for your story, framed as a simulated composite operator.
